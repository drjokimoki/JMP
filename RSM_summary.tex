\section{Theory}

\subsection{Model}

The common-loading model specifies the joint error covariance as
\(\Sige = q\iota_m\iota_m' + D\),
\(D = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1^2,\ldots,\sigma_m^2)\),
precision weights \(\tau_i = \sigma_i^{-2}\).
Two population risk objects are central:

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{L{3.2cm} L{5.4cm} L{6.8cm}}
\rowcolor{myblue}\color{white}\bfseries Object &
\color{white}\bfseries Formula &
\color{white}\bfseries Interpretation \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
\(\Qsub\) & \(q + \mathbb{E}_S[1/A_S]\) & Average-subset risk (\(B\) subsets averaged) \\
\(\Qbaginf\) & \(\bar{v}_k'\Sige\bar{v}_k\) & Infinite-bagging risk (\(G\to\infty\)) \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
\(\Qbag\) & \(\Qbaginf + G^{-1}\!\left(\Qsub - \Qbaginf\right)\) & Finite-bagging bridge — \textbf{exact identity} \\
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Key Theoretical Results}

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{L{3.8cm} L{5.2cm} L{5.4cm}}
\rowcolor{myblue}\color{white}\bfseries Result &
\color{white}\bfseries Formula &
\color{white}\bfseries Assumption \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
Small-het excess risk & \(\Rbag \approx \Cdelta\,\zk^2\) & \(\tau_i \approx \taubar(1+\delta_i)\), \(\delta_i\) small \\
Cube-root optimal \(\kstar\) (bagged) & \(\kstar \approx (2\Cdelta T/A)^{1/3}\) & Small-het \(+\) \(T\gg k\) \(+\) \(\zk\approx 1/k\) \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
Square-root optimal \(\kstar\) (subset) & \(\kstar \approx (A_0 T/B)^{1/2}\) & Leading-order average-subset loss \\
Finite-bagging bridge & \(\Qbag = \Qbaginf + G^{-1}(\Qsub - \Qbaginf)\) & Exact algebraic identity \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
RSM dominance (\(\kappa\)) & \(\Qbaginf < L^{\mathrm{full\,OLS}}\) for \(k\ge\kappa\) & \(\kappa=2\) in all simulated DGPs \\
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Power-Law Generalisation (Addendum, Section 13)}

The small-het formula underestimates the true excess risk.
The addendum proposes \(\Rbag \approx C\cdot\zk^{\alpha}\) and establishes two limit cases:

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{L{3.8cm} L{4.2cm} C{2.4cm} C{2.4cm}}
\rowcolor{myblue}\color{white}\bfseries Regime &
\color{white}\bfseries True form of \(R_k\) &
\color{white}\bfseries Slope \(\ahat\) &
\color{white}\bfseries \(\kstar\) scaling \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
Homoskedastic & \(0\) & --- & any \(k\) optimal \\
Small heterogeneity & \(\Cdelta\,\zk^2\) & \(2\) (exact) & \(T^{1/3}\) \\
\rowcolor{lblue}
Intermediate & Mixture & \(\alpha\in(1,2)\) & \(T^{1/(\alpha+1)}\) \\
Large het (dominant forecaster) & \(C'(k\zk)^2\) & \(\approx 1\) (grid OLS) & \(T^{1/2}\) \\
\end{tabular}

\smallskip\noindent
Generalised optimal \(\kstar = (C\alpha T/A)^{1/(\alpha+1)}\).
A general proof that \(\ahat\in[1,2]\) for arbitrary \(\{\tau_i\}\) \textbf{remains open}
(noted explicitly in the addendum).

\subsection{Small-Het Approximation Quality}

The ratio \(R_k^{\mathrm{mc}}/R_k^{\mathrm{small\text{-}het}}\) grows with \(k\) and blows up near \(k=m\).
Counterintuitively, the approximation is \textbf{worst for \(H=1.05\)}:
the true curve follows \(C'(k\zk)^2\) while the formula gives \(\Cdelta\zk^2\), diverging at rate \(k^2\).
