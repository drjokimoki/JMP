# Review of the theoretical part of `Job_Market_paper_Kozyrev-2.pdf`

Scope: Section 4 (PDF pages 10--23; printed pages 9--22) and its proofs in
Appendices A--G (PDF pages 41--53). The Monte Carlo, Shapley-value, empirical,
and conclusion sections are outside this review.

## Overall assessment

The main population theory is coherent and most of the algebra is correct.
In particular, the constrained optimal-weight formula, the finite-bagging
bridge, the common-loading subset risk, and the infinite-bagged excess-risk
decomposition are valid under the stated positive-definiteness and uniform
subset-sampling conditions.

The section is not yet fully publication-ready. The largest issue is
Proposition 8: its current marginal assumption is imposed only for
`1 << k << m`, but the conclusion concerns a global minimizer over the entire
feasible set. The proof rules out a minimizer in the local interior range; it
does not rule out a later fall in the objective when `k` is comparable with
`m` or `T`. The proposition therefore needs either a stronger global/no-late-
minimum assumption or a conclusion restricted to an interior minimizer.

Several other statements should be presented as approximations or empirical
possibilities rather than consequences of the exact population propositions.

## Valid results

1. **Loss decomposition and benchmarks (Equations 4--6).** For a fixed
   weight vector whose entries sum to one, the conditional MSE is
   `sigma_epsilon^2 + w' Sigma_e w`. The equal-weight and oracle formulas are
   correct.

2. **Finite-bagging bridge (Proposition 1).** For independent subset draws
   and population subset weights,
   `Q_bag,G = Q_bag,infinity + (Q_sub-Q_bag,infinity)/G` is correct. The proof
   needs only independence of the random subset draws and finite second
   moments.

3. **Endpoint/unification result (Proposition 2).** Infinite-bagged RSM gives
   equal weights at `k=1` and oracle weights at `k=m`. This is an endpoint
   identity, not a result about monotonicity or data-driven selection of `k`.

4. **Global/conditional mean-weight identity (Proposition 3).** The identity
   follows directly from the inclusion probability `k/m`. Its interpretation
   must remain about the *mean conditional weight*, not every realized subset
   weight and not standalone forecast accuracy.

5. **Common-loading formulas (Propositions 4 and 6).** The Sherman--Morrison
   calculation, `Q(S)=q+1/A_S`, `w_i,S=tau_i/A_S`, and the weighted-distance
   expression for `R_k^bag` are correct.

6. **Exact bracket (the exact part of Proposition 7).** The Jensen upper
   bound and constrained-projection lower bound are correct when the precision
   profile is heterogeneous. The denominator of the lower bound is positive
   under that condition. The displayed leading-order bracket is an
   approximation and should not be described as an exact inequality.

## Required corrections

### 1. State conditional moments, not an unspecified joint distribution

The display after Assumptions 1--2 uses a distribution symbol even though
only first and second moments have been imposed. It also needs the
`m`-dimensional conditional mean `mu_t iota_m` for `f_t`. Replace it with the
conditional moment display in `theory_revision_for_updated_pdf.tex`.

### 2. Qualify the finite-sample inflation factor

`I(T,k)=(T-1)/(T-k)` is not implied by Assumptions 1--2. It can be motivated
by a particular i.i.d. Gaussian/random-design calculation for one restricted
regression, but it is not the exact risk inflation of feasible bagged RSM:
subset regressions fitted on the same training sample have correlated
coefficient errors. Call the resulting loss a **working or stylized
finite-sample criterion** throughout.

Also keep notation consistent: the text first discusses `I(T,m)` and then
says the factor is generated by estimating `k-1` weights. Use `I(T,d)` for a
generic dimension, followed by `d=m` for the full oracle regression and
`d=k` for a subset regression.

### 3. Separate population subset randomness from estimation randomness

Definitions 1--3 and Proposition 1 use population-optimal subset weights.
They average over random subset selection only. They do not describe the
out-of-sample risk of estimated RSM. Add the clarification supplied in the
revision file after Definition 2.

### 4. Remove overclaims after Proposition 2

Proposition 2 does not show that RSM "detects" homogeneity, selects `k=m`, or
is "not worse than" the two benchmarks. It only identifies the endpoints.
An intermediate `k` can outperform estimated endpoint rules under finite-
sample estimation noise, but that is a possibility under the stylized
criterion and an empirical question, not a consequence of Proposition 2.

### 5. Fix Proposition 5's Taylor status

The exact statement is
`Q_k^sub = q + E_S[1/A_S]`. The next expression is a second-order Taylor
approximation. An `O(k^-3)` remainder requires an explicit asymptotic sequence
and uniform bounds (and should not be asserted merely because precisions are
bounded in one finite panel). Use either:

- the approximation-only version in the revision file, together with its
  relative-dispersion diagnostic; or
- a formal triangular-array proposition with all remainder conditions.

Equation 25 and its optimizer minimize the leading approximation, not the
exact average-subset risk. Label the resulting square-root behavior
accordingly.

### 6. Repair Proposition 8

The current Assumption 4 bounds marginal declines only for `1 << k << m`.
That is insufficient for the stated global `arg min` over
`1 <= k <= min(m,T-1)`: the proof does not exclude a lower objective value
outside the range where the marginal bounds apply. Moreover, constants must
be uniform in the triangular-array index, hence in `T`, `m_T`, and `k`.

The safest correction is the no-late-minimum version in
`theory_revision_for_updated_pdf.tex`. It explicitly restricts attention to
an interior candidate range and assumes that no feasible point outside that
range has lower loss. If a global rate theorem is desired without that
assumption, marginal bounds must instead be imposed over the entire feasible
range in which a competing minimum could occur.

The rate result is conditional and asymptotic. It does not prove that
`round(sqrt(T))` is the exact finite-sample optimum.

## Duplication and presentation

- The endpoint derivation (`k=1` and `k=m`) is given in Section 4.2 and then
  repeated at length after Proposition 6. Keep the algebra in Section 4.2;
  after Proposition 6, retain only one sentence explaining how the
  excess-risk representation reproduces the endpoints.
- The ordering `Q_bag,infinity <= Q_bag,G <= Q_sub` is explained repeatedly
  after Equation 16. One compact paragraph plus the two endpoint cases is
  enough.
- Sections 4.5--4.7 move among exact risk, Taylor approximation, and inflated
  finite-sample criterion without enough signposting. Begin each proposition
  with **Exact**, **Approximation**, or **Working finite-sample criterion**.
- Define one risk notation convention and keep it throughout. At present
  `L`, `Q`, and `R` shift between full MSE, reducible population risk, and
  excess risk. A short notation table would help.
- Replace informal or causal wording such as "RSM detects", "forecast earns
  weight", and "global importance is achieved locally" with literal
  statements about expected weights and risks.
- Correct recurring prose issues: "forecast errors" (not "forecasts errors"),
  "selected" (not "select"), "in practice" (not "on practice"), "Jensen's
  inequality", "heteroskedastic", and "the following form" (not "a following
  form").
- The PDF is legible and equations are not clipped, but visible colored
  hyperlink boxes distract from the theory. Use `hidelinks` or a subdued
  link-color setup for the final version.

## Recommended disposition

Accept Propositions 1--4, 6, and the exact part of Proposition 7 after the
clarifications above. Present Proposition 5 explicitly as a second-order
approximation. Revise Proposition 8 and Appendix G before treating the rate
window as a theorem about the global optimizer.
