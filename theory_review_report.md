# Review of the theoretical part of `Job_Market_paper_Kozyrev-2.pdf`

Scope: Section 4 (pp. 9--21 in the printed pagination) and its proofs in
Appendices A--G. The Monte Carlo, Shapley-value, empirical, and conclusion
sections are outside this review.

## Overall assessment

The central population identities are sound:

- the finite-bagging bridge in Proposition 1;
- the endpoint equivalence of RSM with equal weights at `k=1` and oracle
  weights at `k=m`;
- the global/conditional-weight correspondence;
- the common-loading subset-risk formula `Q(S)=q+1/A_S`;
- the exact representation of infinite-bagged excess risk as a weighted
  squared distance from the oracle weights; and
- the exact upper and lower bounds in Proposition 7.

The section is not yet fully publication-ready. Two claims need substantive
qualification, and the prose and notation need a consistent copy-edit.

## Substantive corrections

### 1. Finite-sample inflation is not distribution-free

The factor `(T-1)/(T-k)` should not be described as the expected
out-of-sample variance inflation without stating the assumptions under which
that expectation is taken. An exact degrees-of-freedom factor of this type
requires a particular regression design/distribution, independence of the
training and evaluation observations, and a precise convention for the
intercept and the `k-1` free weights. In the present paper it is safest to
call the factor a reduced-form approximation.

Suggested replacement after Equation (7):

> Throughout the theoretical comparison, we use
> `I(T,k)=(T-1)/(T-k)` as a reduced-form approximation to the out-of-sample
> inflation generated by estimating `k-1` free combination weights. It is
> exact only under additional assumptions on the regression design and the
> training/evaluation split. Accordingly, the population identities below
> are exact, whereas loss comparisons that multiply by `I(T,k)` are
> conditional on this approximation.

### 2. The rate window is conditional, not implied by the level bounds

The exact level bracket in Proposition 7 does not by itself order the
minimizer of the true loss between the minimizers of its two bounds.
Introducing marginal regularity fixes this logical gap. However, if
`m=m_T` grows with `T`, the constants in that condition must also be uniform
in `m_T`. This matters because the coefficient of the quadratic lower
envelope contains `1/m` and can vanish as the pool grows. The revised text in
`theory_review_replacement.tex` makes the triangular-array requirement and
the conditional nature of the result explicit.

### 3. Clarify what is exact and what is approximate in Proposition 5

Equation (23) is a Taylor approximation, not an exact formula. State the
asymptotic regime for its remainder (for example, bounded precisions and an
interior sequence of subset sizes) and use an approximation sign or write
the remainder explicitly and uniformly. Equation (24) discards a
heterogeneity correction and should be described as leading order only.

## Organization

The definitions of average-subset, finite-bagged, and infinite-bagged risk
are not duplicative. Each object is defined once before Proposition 1, and
the paragraphs following the proposition explain the bridge and its limiting
cases. That interpretation is useful and should remain.

The repeated endpoint discussion after Proposition 6 is also defensible:
Section 4.2 establishes the endpoint identities generally, whereas Section
4.6 shows how the same endpoints appear in the common-loading score
representation. At most, this material could be shortened for pace; it is
not a logical or structural defect.

The only optional compression concerns the two boundary optimizers. The
main text can state their rate intuition briefly while Appendix G retains
the derivations, but keeping both is acceptable if the paper is intended to
be self-contained for readers who do not immediately consult the appendix.

## Presentation and notation

- Use one term consistently: "equal weighting" rather than alternating
  among averaging, average weighting, and equal weighting.
- Reserve `Q` for forecast-error population risk and `L` for total loss
  including `sigma_epsilon^2` and any finite-sample factor. This distinction
  is conceptually useful and should be stated once.
- Use `\Sigma_{e,m}` consistently; Section 4.3 contains `\Sigma_{e.m}`.
- Use `P_S`, not both `P_s` and `P_S`.
- Define `\bar\tau`, `V_\tau`, `V_\delta`, `B`, and `H` in one notation
  block. Avoid redefining them in later subsections.
- Correct "forecast precision of a forecast", "hetereskedastic",
  "conditional on being select", "On practice", "a following form",
  "depends scales", and similar grammatical errors.
- Replace smart quotation marks in LaTeX source and standardize equation
  punctuation.

## Recommended revised material

`theory_review_replacement.tex` provides corrected replacement text for the
exact bounds and rate-window subsection and the corresponding appendix
proofs. It preserves the valid exact bracket, explicitly separates level
bounds from marginal assumptions, and states the extra uniformity needed
for the asymptotic rate claim.
